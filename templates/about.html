About the Microbial Digital Library

A self-hosted web application for keeping a microbial culture collection in order: strain records, plate photographs, 3D colony models, Gram stain history, revival schedules, freezer positions and printable labels. It was built for brewing microbiology laboratories and works for any lab that keeps a library of frozen strains. Version {{ version }}.

What you can do with it

Strain records

Genus, species and strain, accession numbers from other collections (NCYC, DSM, ATCC and others), where the sample came from, tags, brewing role and risk. Add your own fields for anything else your lab needs.

Revival tracking

Set a revival interval for each strain. The due date is calculated and shown as green, amber or red. Every check is kept with its date, who did it, the result and the colony count entered.

Gram stain history

Dated results (positive, negative, variable or not done) with an optional micrograph, so a strain can be re-assessed over time without losing earlier results.

Plate photographs

Each photo has its own medium, incubation conditions and capture details. Add several plates from one session at once, and correct any detail later.

3D models

Build a 3D model from a plate photograph, or generate one from a strain's cell and colony shape. View it in the browser or download an STL file for 3D printing.

Storage and labels

Record freezer, rack, box and position. Two strains in one spot are flagged. Print labels with a QR code that opens the strain's page from a phone.

Import and export

Import an existing Excel or CSV strain list after previewing it. Export everything as a ZIP of CSV files or an Excel workbook at any time.

Shared labs

Several labs can use one installation, each with its own private catalog. Colleagues join a lab with its join code and an admin's approval.

How your data is handled

  • Where it is stored. Everything is stored on the computer or server that runs this installation: one database file, plus your photographs and 3D models. If this installation runs on a rented or cloud server, your data is on that server and in the care of its provider. Ask whoever runs it which it is.
  • Who receives it. The authors of the software never receive your data and cannot see it. Nothing is sent anywhere else.
  • Who can see it. Only people with an account in this lab, which a lab admin approves. Whoever manages the computer or server can also open the files directly, so choose who runs it with care.
  • Messages. A message sent from the Contact page goes to this lab's admins, not to the authors of the software.
  • Passwords are stored as salted hashes. Accounts lock for a short time after repeated wrong passwords.
  • You can export all of your data whenever you like, and an administrator can take a full backup with the included backup script. Exports and backups are copies that you must keep safe yourselves.

Responsibility for the data

  • This installation is run by whoever set it up. They, not the authors of the software, are responsible for the data stored here, for who can reach it, and for backups.
  • The authors have no access to this installation and cannot see or recover its data.
  • The software is free and comes with no warranty. The authors are not liable for lost or wrongly recorded data, unauthorised access or leaks. The full terms are in the MIT licence that comes with the software.
  • Take regular backups and keep them somewhere other than the computer that runs the app.

What it does not do

  • It does not count colonies automatically. Counts are entered by a person with each revival check, because automatic counts are unreliable when colonies touch.
  • The plate analysis estimates how much of the dish is covered and the general look of the growth. It does not identify species or contamination.
  • 3D models are for viewing, teaching and printing. They are not calibrated measurements of a colony.
  • Names recorded against actions (who added a strain, who did a revival) are typed in by the operator. They are a record, not proof of identity.
  • It is a practical record-keeping tool for a lab. It does not replace the procedures of an accredited culture collection.

Who can do what

RoleWhat they can do
UserAdd and edit strains, log plates, Gram stains and revival checks, and void a wrong entry with a reason.
Lab adminEverything a user can do, plus manage accounts, approve people who ask to join, set custom fields, import and export data, delete a strain and read messages from the Contact page.

Each installation holds one lab. To join it, ask a lab admin for the join code, then ask to join.

Technical details

Version {{ version }}. Built with Python and Flask, a single SQLite database file, and OpenCV, NumPy, SciPy and Pillow for image analysis. The 3D viewer is bundled with the application, so it works without an internet connection. It runs on Windows, macOS and Linux, and any device on the same network (including phones) can use it through a browser.
